Concept Stage

SMARA

The Unfinished City — an RTS of the Saharan Resistance

An asymmetric real-time strategy game of the Mauritanian resistance to French colonization, 1902–1934. No base building. Wells, herds and tribal allegiance are the war.

The Pitch

Between 1902 and 1934, France absorbed a desert country the size of France and Spain combined — with almost no battles. Corsican Arabist Xavier Coppolani took three emirates with treaties and sermons; when a single rezzou killed him at Tidjikja in 1905, the conquest froze for three years, and the Sahara answered with the oldest weapon it had: the lightning camel raid.

SMARA is a point-control RTS with no worker economy and no base building. The map is a lattice of wells, oases, pastures and caravan routes. Whoever controls the water controls the war: away from a friendly well, every unit bleeds a thirst meter until it dies — thirteen tirailleurs fell at Nouarmach in July 1908, and eleven more died of thirst afterward. That statistic is a game mechanic.

The two sides fight for the same territory with opposite tools. The French side webs a lattice of mud-brick forts (borj) across the country, extends its supply radius post by post, and budgets units from Saint-Louis — reinforcements that can be ambushed in transit. The resistance side lives on mobility: rezzou raids that strike herds and caravans and vanish, tribal allegiances that must be won and held, and charismatic leaders whose presence is worth a regiment — until their death breaks one.

A pre-colonial prologue era teaches the world before the flag arrives: the emirates' own wars, the gum and salt trade, the rules of the raid. Then the player meets France not as a protagonist but as a disruption — a world at war with itself, invaded.

Gameplay Pillars

Wells Are Resources

Capture points are wells, oases, palmaries and caravan stops. Deny a well to an enemy column and you kill it slowly — no assault required.

Herds Are Economy

Camels and cattle graze controlled pasture. Wealth walks — it can be raided, driven off, and taken back. The azalaï salt caravans are resource convoys on legs.

Allegiance Is Recruitment

No barracks, no unit spam. Each tribe that swears to you is a manpower pool with its own flavor — Rguibat raiders fight nothing like Kounta levies. Lose the tribe's allegiance, lose the army.

The Borj Lattice

France builds no cities either. A web of small desert forts extends supply radius and safe water, post by post — Nouakchott, Tidjikja, Moudjeria, Atar. Cut the web and the desert returns.

The Rezzou

The lightning raid: converge, strike, take herds and captives, and be gone before pursuit can mount. France's answer was the contre-rezzou — the same tactic with a flag.

Thirst

Every unit carries water. Distance from a friendly well is a decay timer. Heat is the third army in every battle, and it never negotiates.

The Campaign

Act 0 · Prologue

Before the Flag

17th century – 1901

The world before invasion: emirates and marabouts, gum trade down the Senegal river, salt caravans across the erg. The tutorial era — master the rezzou, the wells and the tribute, because every one of those tools is about to be aimed at an empire.

Act I

The Charmeur

1902 – 12 May 1905

Xavier Coppolani conquers Trarza, Brakna and Tagant with treaties, subsidies and sermons — "penetrate by speech, not by rifle." It ends in a palmery at Tidjikja: a dream-vow assassin, twenty-seven warriors, five minutes after night prayer. The conquest freezes for three years.

Act II

The Column

1905 – 1909

The harassment war: rezzous bleed the occupier column by column — Niémilane, Damané, Nouarmach and its eleven dead of thirst. Colonel Gouraud answers with camel columns and the counter-rezzou, forces the Hamdoun pass, and takes Atar on 9 January 1909.

Act III

The Long Decay

1910 – 1934

Ma El Aïnin's field army dies at Tadla in Morocco and the saint himself follows — but the desert is not done. Smara burns in 1913 and vengeance answers at Liboirat, Tagliat and Bou-Telis. A puppet emir defects, a méhariste company deserts, and the last great Rguibat rezzou rides 6,000 kilometers before the "pacification" closes at Tindouf, 31 May 1934.

About the Project

SMARA is a concept-stage game design. Target engine: Godot 4 (open source, no royalties). Single-player campaign first; no release date exists. Design docs, the historical research backbone and the character bible live in this repository.

Historical basis

The campaign follows the documented record of the French pacification of Mauritania. Where sources disagree — siege dates, the year an emir was captured — the disagreements are flagged in the research document rather than smoothed over. One caution from the research is baked into the design: the war's true asymmetry was logistics and water, not weapons — both sides carried comparable rifles.
