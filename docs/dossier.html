Core Design Document v0.1

The Game Design

Godot 4 · point-control RTS, no base-building (World in Conflict loop, Northgard zones) · campaign 1480–1934. Every mechanic that is invention, not history, carries a design fiction badge in this dossier.

Asymmetry is economy, not stats

Both sides field similar small arms — the gap is logistics and forts, not weapons quality. The resistance takes what it fields; France pulls what it fields across an empire. Every unit deployed is cargo somebody moved across the map.

Mobility vs lattice

Camel speed and well knowledge vs the borj lattice and camel columns. France buys reach by building; the resistance is reach.

Water is the clock

Every unit ticks thirst away from friendly wells. Timers, not health bars, decide desert operations — Nouarmach: 13 killed in action, 11 died of thirst after.

Allegiance is production

No barracks. Manpower pools live in tribes; tribes switch sides. Losing allegiance = losing the ability to make war. 800+ recognized chiefs, divide-and-rule.

The Two Economies

Take — the resistance

loot, tribute, herds
  • Resources: loot crates (arms/ammo/supply), camels, herds, water, baraka (morale)
  • Loop: rezzou → loot → rearm → bigger rezzou
  • Sinks: raid-range extension, war parties, gifts to tribes (allegiance)

Pull — France

imperial logistics, subsidies
  • Resources: military budget, administrative budget, water, convoys
  • Loop: budget → convoy → borj → projection → submissions → income
  • Sinks: columns, borj construction, subsidies, escort

The two loops interlock into one transfer economy: zero-sum cargo. Every crate looted is a crate France must re-pull from the metropole through two colonies. Looting isn't flavor — it is the opponent's supply line bleeding.

Looting — the resistance core loop

Loot drops as physical crates on raid sites and must be carried off on camels — loot has weight and slows the band. Categories: arms (Gras rifles until the real 1907 tech event, then Berthier Mle 1907s; machine-guns only as rare column loot), ammunition (the true bottleneck — rifles without cartridges are clubs), provisions & water gear (+raid range), pack camels (the loot multiplier), military kit, coin/valuables (convertible to allegiance via the zawiya network).

A war band's tier is its equipment, not its experience: lance band → Gras band (10 looted rifles + ammo) → rifle line (30+ rifles, sustained volley fire). The tech tree reads as a capture log. Looted rifles wear (no armourer network) and ammo is consumed, never manufactured — every volley fired shrinks the war. Artillery and machine guns can be captured but never served: no ammunition pipeline, no trained crews. design fiction looted-kit disguise (one band per raid passes as a friendly méhariste patrol at distance); design fiction telegraph-wire reuse as camp alarm nets.

The French imperial pull

A physical, visible, attackable supply lane: metropole (budget accrues, tier-limited by year) → Saint-Louis (river steamers; rail head later) → forward depot (Rosso/Podor → Moudjeria) → convoy (camel trains, slow, escort-dependent; each borj passed refreshes escort) → borj lattice (supply radius projects water discipline, reinforcement spawn, treaty projection). Interception points: river dockings (safe, tier-limited), convoy legs (the resistance's hunting ground), borj handoff (garrisoned).

Budget duality: military budget buys columns, garrisons, escorts, borjs; administrative budget buys subsidies, treaty missions, emir restorations — submissions convert tribes to income but cost real time and diplomats, and losing a restored emir to dissidence (the Ayda arc) is an administrative catastrophe. Escalation tiers by year: 1902–05 diplomacy-cheap/military-poor (Coppolani) → 1907–09 column war (Gouraud) → 1910–13 peak projection → WWI scarcity (budget halved; the 1916 German gun-run event) → 1920s–34 police war (élite méharistes, slow, cheap).

Shared systems

  • Wells — capture points; denial by filling/scattering (fast) or corpse-poisoning (slow, permanent, atrocity-marked — design fiction the poisoning method; atrocity mechanics cost allegiance globally).
  • Thirst clock — ticks up off-well; penalty → attrition → death (the Nouarmach rule). Columns decay slower inside borj radius.
  • Herds — mobile slow economy, graze seasonal pasture, raidable; herd loss = famine events in tribes.
  • Caravan economy (azalaï) — neutral salt convoys on fixed calendar routes; tax (income, routes reroute away) or raid (loot, route dies). France taxing = submission pressure; resistance raiding hurts civilian tribes.
  • Allegiance meter (per tribe, −100…+100) — drivers: subsidies, gifts, protection efficacy, atrocity proximity, marabout diplomacy (Sidiya Baba = French-side multiplier; Goudfiya sheikhs = resistance-side), defeat of local rivals.
  • Seasons — rainy season (Jun–Sep): Sahel pasture blooms, columns bog, rezzou season pauses; dry season: the water war peaks; transhumance windows move the herd targets.

Unit rosters (sketch)

Resistance

taken arms, tribal pools
  • Lance band → Gras band → rifle line
  • Rezzou raiders (fast, loot-bonus, fragile)
  • Herd drivers (civilian logistics)
  • Marabout retinue (baraka aura — no rout, allegiance diplomacy)
  • Tribal levies (quality = tribe allegiance)
  • Tbal drum unit (capturable objective — sovereign morale field)

France

pulled arms, imperial budget
  • Tirailleur foot company; camel-mounted tirailleurs
  • Méhariste company (élite, water-discipline aura, era-correct khaki + chéchia)
  • Spahis (river-theatre cavalry)
  • 2e escadron de spahis algériens — rare veteran cavalry, confirmed in the 1908–09 colonnes volantes
  • Borj garrison; engineer escort; telegraph crew (vision link between borjs)
  • Light mountain gun (2×65mm Mle 1906 per column) + machine-gun section; diplomats / Coppolani-type agents

Neutral: azalaï caravan, zawiya (safe-passage node), tribal camps (both sides).

Seven mission archetypes

Rezzou raid

Win: loot quota + extraction off-map. Lose: band destroyed / thirst death.

Post siege

Win: borj falls or garrison surrenders. Lose: relief column arrives unbled.

Column escort

Win: convoy % delivered. Lose: convoy burned.

Submission map

Win: tribes above threshold before budget runs. Lose: rebellion spreads.

Counter-rezzou

Win: destroy raider band. Lose: raiders escape with loot.

Assassination window

Win: target killed in the 5-minute window. Lose: window closes. (Scripted, resistance.)

Waterless crossing

Win: cross the gap with X% alive. Either faction.

Balance levers

  • Looting snowball — cap carried ammo per band (camels = soft cap); French tier escalation floods better-armed escorts; ammunition burn rate per volley.
  • French turtling — administrative income requires submissions, which require projection; borj upkeep drains military budget. The lattice is a money fire, not a fort line.
  • Well camping — denial is cheaper than holding; a well denied to both sides becomes dead ground; season shifts move the fight.
  • Thirst death-spiral — bands auto-path via known wells / supply radius: player error punished, pathing error never.
  • Ledger transparency — France seeing "240 crates lost" creates counter-raid pressure organically.
Working Draft v1 · 16 Missions, 4 Acts

The Campaign

Quotes used are documented lines only; all invention is tagged in the design's fiction register. The crest marks the Shinqit / resistance player, the tricolor the French player; Act 0's crested sides are the pre-war emirates and confederations.

ACT 0 — The Old War · 1480–1900 · tutorial

Teach the world before France exists: wells, tribute, caravans, allegiance — the exact systems Act I will weaponize.

ACT I — The Man of Speech · 1902–1905 · France

The French player learns conquest without battle: budget, posts, submissions.

ACT II — The Harassment War · 1905–1909 · both

The war becomes what the desert does best: attrition of water, nerve, and supply.

ACT III — The Long Decay · 1910–1934 · both

Armies die; the war outlives them. Endgame is administrative — and the soul of the ending is: never defeated on Mauritanian soil.

The fiction register (11 items) is the contract: history supplies the outcomes, fiction supplies the play. Full register and mechanic-coverage matrix in CAMPAIGN_SCRIPTS.md.

Character Bible · 12 Profiles

The Full Cast

Xavier Coppolani

1866 – 1905

"Le Charmeur." The diplomat-general: no combat stats — his aura converts tribes to protectorate treaties without fighting. Assassinated 12 May 1905; his death flips French doctrine from diplomacy to columns.

France · Hero→NPC

Col. Henri Gouraud

1867 – 1946

The Column. Buffs camel columns — march speed, water discipline; his Contre-rezzou ability mirrors the raid's own tactics. Unlocks the méhariste companies.

France · Hero

Capt. Frèrejean

fl. 1904 – 1909

The Post-Builder. Founded Nouakchott in 1904; led the 1909 reinforcements. The borj lattice personified — "link the forts" missions are his.

France · NPC

Lt. Etiévent

1905

The Avenger. Kills Coppolani's assassin with two revolver shots. A single cinematic cameo — and the optional duel against Sidi Seghir.

France · NPC

Cheikh Ma El Aïnin

1831 – 1910

The Saint of Smara. Not a combat unit — a living wonder: his Jihad aura rallies tribes and raises levies; Baraka makes nearby units never rout. His death starts a faction-collapse timer. Died at Tiznit "undefeated on Mauritanian soil."

Resistance · Hero

Sidi Seghir Ould Moulay Zein

d. 1905

The Dream-Vow. One mission, one window: the milk delivery, 21h25–21h30. Historical outcome: 8 of 20 attackers die; he falls to Etiévent's revolver.

Resistance · Hero (M8)

Ahmed al-Hiba

c. 1876 – 1919

The Blue Sultan. Highest levy multiplier in the cast — the Sultan's Banner raises mass armies fast (10,000 at Marrakech) at poor unit quality. Took a city; lost an empire in three weeks.

Resistance · Hero

Agdaf

fl. 1913

The Vengeance. After Smara burns, his Reprisal ability grants critical-hit bonuses against posts — the dark middle of Act III, tone-governed: the dead are named, never rewarded.

Resistance · Hero

Merebbi Rebbu

d. after 1919

The Remnant. Successor from 1919, holder of the Saguia el-Hamra rump — quest-giver of the late Reguibat raids: Port-Étienne, the grand rezzou of 1934.

Resistance · NPC

Emir Sidi Ahmed Ould Ahmed Ayda

r. 1909 – 1932

The Puppet. Installed, captured, restored as a hollow French instrument, defected to dissidence, died in the desert 1932 — the emirate ends with him. His allegiance meter swings the whole Adrar's rules.

Adrar · NPC

Cheikh Saad Bouh

1848 – 1917

The Other Brother. Pro-French pacifist marabout, Ma El Aïnin's brother — same father, same brotherhood, opposite war. Wrote the Nasîha: "He who takes arms departs from virtue." Campaign narrator candidate.

Mediator · NPC

Cheikh Sidiya Baba

Boutilimit

The Kingmaker. Head of the Qadiriyya-Sidiyya, Coppolani's principal Mauritanian ally — the "peaceful penetration" tech tree in human form. French-side allegiance multiplier.

Ally of France · NPC
