## ACT 0 — THE OLD WAR (1480–1900 · tutorial)
*Teach the world before France exists: wells, tribute, caravans, allegiance — the exact systems Act I will weaponize.*

### M1 — TONDIBI
**Act 0 · 13 March 1591 · Niger bend** · **Songhai (Askia Ishaq's host)** · Archetype: field battle (asymmetric tech)
**Setup:** Judar Pasha's Moroccan expedition has crossed the 4-month desert — you command the Songhai side at Tondibi. Your host dwarfs the invaders: cavalry wings, 1,000 cattle herded as a screen [RPC].
**Objectives:** Primary: destroy the Moroccan vanguard. Secondary: keep the cattle screen intact. Hidden: survive 20 minutes with the Askia alive (the battle is unwinnable by design — the lesson is the tech cliff).
**Scripted events:** t0 — arquebus volleys break the first charge; t+8min — cattle stampede turns back onto your own lines (the documented panic); t+15min — Askia's departure triggers army collapse.
**Intro text:** *Narration.* "In the year 999 of the Hijra, a renegade pasha led four thousand men across the sands our caravans measure in months. They carry tubes of iron that speak with fire."
**Outro text:** *Narration.* "The empire of Songhai did not die at Tondibi of its wounds — it died of the lesson: the gun had come south, and nothing that ignored it would long remain."
**New mechanic:** unit morale + the firearms-vs-mass balance rule (learnt by losing).
**History anchors:** RCH §1.1 background; RPC §1 Tondibi, 13 Mar 1591. Cattle-stampede beat [RPC, VERIFIED].

### M2 — THE PROPHET OF THE LANCE (Char Bouba finale)
**Act 0 · 1670s · Tagant/Trarza** · **Berber znaga confederation** · Archetype: survival + allegiance map
**Setup:** The Char Bouba war (1644–74) ends in defeat, but the peace builds the world: you hold the line as Nasr ad-Din's successors fall one by one [RPC].
**Objectives:** Primary: extract the zawaya refugees and the library to safety (mobile convoy). Secondary: bind surviving tribes into the post-war hierarchy (hassane/znaga pact). Hidden: preserve the tribute law — milk, a third of well water, three days' hospitality [RPC].
**Scripted events:** each 5 minutes a named successor-leader dies (Tirtillass, Tin Yijmara, Amadir sequence [RPC]); final beat — treaty screen codifies the social order as game rules.
**Intro text:** *Proclamation (tbal).* "The drum of the war is heavier than a man. Carry it, and you carry what remains of us." (after RPY tbal prose — drum capture "weightier than killing" [VERIFIED]).
**Outro text:** *Narration.* "The warriors would take tribute; the scholars would take respect. Between them they built a nation of tents — the order your grandchildren will defend two centuries hence."
**New mechanic:** tribal allegiance meter + tribute economy.
**History anchors:** RPC §Char Bouba; dating conflict noted RPC (1644–74 used, revisionist battles as finale per RPC recommendation).

### M3 — THE SALT ROAD
**Act 0 · c.1800 · Timbuktu–Taoudeni axis** · **Kunta caravan masters** · Archetype: escort (caravan economy)
**Setup:** The azalaï loads 25-kg salt slabs, two to four to a camel, three weeks out and back [RPC]. Rezzou riders shadow the route.
**Objectives:** Primary: deliver the salt to Timbuktu. Secondary: scout and pre-empt three ambush wadis. Hidden: bribe one shadowing band into escort duty (turn raider into guard).
**Scripted events:** night 6 — well fight; night 12 — dust sign (choice: camp hidden or push thirsty); night 18 — the ransom negotiation.
**Intro text:** *Caravan ledger.* "Twice a year we go. The salt buys millet, cloth, peace. The desert takes its share in water and its price in blood."
**Outro text:** *Narration.* "Every caravan taxes the future: each safe run teaches the raiders exactly where the next one will pass."
**New mechanic:** caravan/trade lane income (azalaï) — neutral resource both factions can later tax or raid.
**History anchors:** RPC §azalaï; GP §5 caravan economy.

### M4 — THE TWO MONOPOLIES
**Act 0 · 1820s–1850s · Trarza river belt** · **Trarza emirate** · Archetype: diplomacy map (treachery)
**Setup:** Saint-Louis merchants want the gum; so do the Dutch. You are the amir. Sell the same monopoly to both; collect the coutume from both [RPC].
**Objectives:** Primary: maximize gum income across 10 years without open war. Secondary: arm the emirate with imported rifles. Hidden: when the fraud is discovered, choose your enemy — and keep the rifles either way.
**Scripted events:** year 4 — French naval demonstration at the river mouth; year 7 — Dutch envoy matches the French offer; year 10 — exposure, ultimatum, choice screen.
**Intro text:** *Letter (narrated translation).* "Tell the Christians of the river: the acacia grows on my land, and the wind that dries its tears blows for no flag."
**Outro text:** *Narration.* "Gum built the emirates' arsenals — and taught two empires that the north bank was worth taking. Faidherbe is already a cadet officer in Saint-Louis."
**New mechanic:** diplomacy/treaty system + trade-income treachery.
**History anchors:** RCH §1.1 (1825 crisis, gum trade, arms sales by Saint-Louis merchants); RPC §gum market tutorial.

## ACT I — THE MAN OF SPEECH (1902–1905 · France)
*The French player learns conquest without battle: budget, posts, submissions.*

### M5 — PAROLE, PAS FUSIL
**Act I · 1903 · Trarza** · **France (Coppolani)** · Archetype: submission map
**Setup:** Paris has adopted Coppolani's plan: "penetrate by speech, not by rifle" [RCH §1.2]. The map is neutral tribes; your treasury is administrative, not military.
**Objectives:** Primary: bring Trarza to protectorate via marabout alliances (Sidiya Baba). Secondary: found one post. Hidden: keep military spending at zero (the doctrine, obeyed, is the achievement).
**Scripted events:** intervention — a Saint-Louis merchant offers to sell YOU arms; refusing earns Sidiya Baba's trust.
**Intro text:** *Dispatch, Coppolani.* "In Muslim lands, the word religion replaces the word patrie." [RCH §2.1, documented]
**Outro text:** *Narration.* "Trarza submits without a shot. In Paris they begin to speak of a Corsican who conquers with sermons."
**New mechanic:** administrative budget + subsidy/treaty toolset.
**History anchors:** RCH §1.2 (1901 plan, 1903 Trarza).

### M6 — THE LATTICE
**Act I · 1904 · Brakna–coast** · **France (Frèrejean)** · Archetype: supply-line builder
**Setup:** Build posts across the south — Nouakchott among them [RCH §1.2] — and keep them watered from the Senegal baseline.
**Objectives:** Primary: link three borjs into an unbroken supply radius. Secondary: keep every convoy un-raided. Hidden: a silent well between posts (a rider dies of thirst if you don't scout).
**Scripted events:** post 2 completion triggers first dissident probe; a convoy route auto-plots through a rezzou corridor if you don't override.
**Intro text:** *Engineer's notebook.* "A fort is a promise: that the flag outlives the man who planted it. Water is the ink."
**Outro text:** *Narration.* "The lattice grows north. Each borj is a pin through the map — and through something older that did not ask to be pinned."
**New mechanic:** borj lattice + supply radius extension (GP §4).
**History anchors:** RCH §1.2 (Frèrejean, Nouakchott 1904); GP §4.

### M7 — TAGANT
**Act I · 1905 · Tidjikja** · **France (Coppolani)** · Archetype: submission map + seed of war
**Setup:** Extend to the Tagant, plant Tidjikja. The map reads peaceful — but a jihad has been proclaimed from the unfinished city of Smara [RCH §1.2, 1904].
**Objectives:** Primary: Tagant protectorate. Secondary: fund the palmery and the post. Hidden: a dreamer is watching the post's evening routine — unseen, unnarrated (pays off in M8).
**Scripted events:** Ma El Aïnin's envoy crosses the map edge without engaging; marabout counsel warns the Tagant is "subscribed, not persuaded."
**Intro text:** *Letter, Saad Bouh to his brother — the Nasîha is coming:* "He who takes arms departs from virtue." [RCH §1.3, 1906 letter condensed forward — [FICTION] timing]
**Outro text:** *Narration.* "The Tagant signs. Somewhere in the palm-shade of Tidjikja, twenty-seven men learn the post's evening order by heart."
**New mechanic:** warning-signs layer (allegiance is not loyalty).
**History anchors:** RCH §1.2 (Tagant 1905, Tidjikja); jihad proclamation 1904.

### M8 — THE MILK DELIVERY
**Act I close · 12 May 1905 · Tidjikja palmery** · **RESISTANCE (Sidi Seghir, 27 Tenaki)** · Archetype: assassination window
**Setup:** Night, after Isha prayer. Coppolani walks the palmery at the milk delivery, 21h25–21h30 — a five-minute window [RCH §1.2].
**Objectives:** Primary: kill Coppolani inside the window. Secondary: extract Sidi Seghir alive (history: he dies — Lt. Etiévent's revolver; choose: honor history or the hidden objective). Hidden: spare the escort tirailleurs (they are Senegalese levies, not the target).
**Scripted events:** 21h20 milk detail forms; 21h25 window opens; alarm at first shot; Lt. Etiévent duels Sidi Seghir if extraction attempted.
**Intro text:** *Narration over the sharif's dream.* "He had seen this palmery in sleep: the milk, the lantern, the man in the cream suit who talked a country into sleep. Twenty-seven answered. Twenty would go."
**Outro text:** *Narration + documented last words.* Coppolani, shot in the back: "These wretches have killed me — they do not deserve me." [RCH §1.2]. "The advance France had planned for a decade froze for three years."
**New mechanic:** stealth + timed-window operations.
**History anchors:** RCH §1.2 (entire engagement documented, 8 of 20 attackers lost, one survivor hanged).

## ACT II — THE HARASSMENT WAR (1905–1909 · both, alternating)
*The war becomes what the desert does best: attrition of water, nerve, and supply.*

### M9 — THE REZZOU ECONOMY
**Act II · 1906 · Tagant** · **RESISTANCE (Goudfiya raiders)** · Archetype: raid loop (looting tutorial)
**Setup:** Gouraud has not yet replaced persuasion with columns; posts stand thin. Your war-band must LIVE off the enemy: no manufacture, only taking [GP §3].
**Objectives:** Primary: loot three convoys (rifles, ammo, rations, camels). Secondary: re-arm a lance band to Gras rifles. Hidden: leave the tirailleure escort a water barrel (mercy pays allegiance dividends later).
**Scripted events:** first successful loot unlocks the equipment-tier pipeline; over-raiding one tribe's route drops its allegiance.
**Intro text:** *Camp proverb.* "My day is not the day of idle men; my day is the day gunpowder burns." — al-Kounti's kāf against Coppolani [RPY, ATTRIBUTED-oral]
**Outro text:** *Narration.* "Every crate that crossed the sea for the columns now rides north on captured camels. The ledger balances in blood and gunpowder."
**New mechanic:** looting + repurposing (GP §3 core).
**History anchors:** RCH §1.3 harassment war; RPY kāf.

### M10 — THE THIRST CLOCK
**Act II · 24 July 1908 · Nouarmach wells** · **RESISTANCE** · Archetype: well-denial
**Setup:** A French column marches the waterless gap; you cannot beat it in the field — you can make the desert drink it [RCH §1.3: 13 killed in combat, 11 more died of thirst after].
**Objectives:** Primary: deny/hold three wells along the column's axis. Secondary: ambush the watering parties (never the full column). Hidden: poison nothing — deny by presence, keep the wells drinkable for the tribes (allegiance).
**Scripted events:** column's thirst meter visible to player; hour 20 — first cattle die; hour 30 — watering-party ambush window; end-state replays the documented 11 dead of thirst.
**Intro text:** *Narration.* "You cannot out-shoot a column. But a column is a herd: it drinks where you permit, when you permit, or it dies walking."
**Outro text:** *Narration.* "Thirteen fell to bullets at Nouarmach. Eleven more fell to the hours after — the number France carried home was the desert's, not ours."
**New mechanic:** thirst attrition + well control as weapon (GP §5).
**History anchors:** RCH §1.3 Nouarmach 24 Jul 1908 (documented figures).

### M11 — FORT COPPOLANI
**Act II · 5–30 November 1906 · Tidjikja** · **France (post garrison)** · Archetype: siege defense
**Setup:** The month-long siege of the Tidjikja post [RCH §1.3 — conflict note: possible second siege Nov 1907; one siege dramatized]. A mixed garrison, thin walls, the Tagant dissident around you.
**Objectives:** Primary: hold the post 25 days. Secondary: ration water without mutiny; keep the tricolor flying (the flag is a morale object). Hidden: sally at night to burn the besiegers' date stores (hastens the lift).
**Scripted events:** day 9 — water critical, ration choice; day 17 — relief column sighted, feint; day 24 — siege lifts (documented).
**Intro text:** *Garrison log.* "They took the man who built this post in his palmery. Now they test whether they can take his shadow."
**Outro text:** *Narration.* "The post held. In Paris they learned what a borj costs; in the Tagant they learned what a borj cannot buy."
**New mechanic:** siege endurance + rationing.
**History anchors:** RCH §1.3 (5–30 Nov 1906, Capt. Tissot; conflict register #1).

### M12 — THE SEVEN-HUNDRED-CAMEL COLUMN
**Act II · Dec 1908–Jan 1909 · Moudjeria → Hamdoun → Atar** · **France (Gouraud)** · Archetype: column logistics
**Setup:** The Colonne de l'Adrar: 700+ camels of logistics, tirailleurs mounted à dos de chameau [RCH §1.4]. Not conquest, Gouraud says — "a police column to purge the country" [RCH §2.1, documented].
**Objectives:** Primary: force the Hamdoun pass (8 Jan) and occupy Atar (9 Jan) — the two documented dates. Secondary: cross the Moudjeria–Adrar gap without losing a man to thirst. Hidden: accept the submission of a dissident clan on the road (alive > dead for the administrative ledger).
**Scripted events:** Amatil affair en route (30–31 Dec, 4 killed [RCH]); pass-forcing battle; Atar occupation + protectorate declaration screen.
**Intro text:** *Gouraud, order of the day.* "It is not a matter of conquering the Adrar for permanent occupation… but of organizing a police column to purge the country." [RCH §2.1]
**Outro text:** *Narration.* "Nine January: Atar. The emir flees; the plateau opens. France has its foothold — and a war of a different shape."
**New mechanic:** column marching + water discipline doctrine (GP §4).
**History anchors:** RCH §1.4 (dates, camel count, quotes documented).

## ACT III — THE LONG DECAY (1910–1934 · both)
*Armies die; the war outlives them. Endgame is administrative — and the soul of the ending is: never defeated on Mauritanian soil.*

### M13 — TADLA
**Act III · 23 June 1910 · Kasba Zidania, Morocco** · **RESISTANCE (Ma El Aïnin)** · Archetype: lost battle (tragedy)
**Setup:** The sheikh's field army — 5,000–6,000 men raised to take Marrakech and Fès [RCH §1.5]. It is the resistance's last conventional host, and it must break.
**Objectives:** Primary (rewriting is impossible — the mission is the retreat): extract Ma El Aïnin wounded from the field. Secondary: save the manuscript chests and the banner. Hidden: disperse, not annihilate — save 2,000 of the 5,000 (they become Act III's raider pool).
**Scripted events:** Aubert's artillery opens on cue; the sheikh is wounded (documented); sons rally the retreat; final shot — the host dissolving into the Anti-Atlas.
**Intro text:** *Narration.* "At seventy-nine he marched the width of two deserts to raise the Souss. What answered him was the army of a nation of forty million."
**Outro text:** *Narration.* "Broken at Tadla, he carried the cause to Tiznit, where he proclaimed the Mahdiship — and where, on 23 October 1910, he died. Undefeated on Mauritanian soil." [RCH §1.5]
**New mechanic:** retreat/extraction operations; loss missions as a designed experience.
**History anchors:** RCH §1.5 (Tadla 23 Jun, death 23 Oct 1910).

### M14 — THE BLUE SULTAN
**Act III · 14 Aug – 6 Sep 1912 · Marrakech → Sidi Bou Othmane** · **RESISTANCE (El Hiba)** · Archetype: victory that cannot hold
**Setup:** El Hiba enters Marrakech with 10,000 men and is proclaimed Sultan [RCH §1.5]. Mangin is three weeks away.
**Objectives:** Primary: hold Marrakech 23 days (history: you cannot hold past day 23 — 6 Sep is fixed). Secondary: evacuate the treasury and the arms store before the fall. Hidden: get the urban collaborators out with you (they hang otherwise — unshown, narrated).
**Scripted events:** day 1 — proclamation; day 20 — Mangin's spahis sighted; day 23 — Sidi Bou Othmane: ~2,000 dead, the fixed defeat; extraction epilogue.
**Intro text:** *Proclamation, narrated.* "The son of the saint of Smara is crowned in the Red City. Ten thousand spears follow him. The newspapers of two empires mispronounce his name."
**Outro text:** *Narration.* "Two thousand men died in an afternoon at Sidi Bou Othmane. The Sultan of three weeks rode south, and the war went home to the tents, where it could not be beheaded."
**New mechanic:** time-limited hold + evacuation logistics.
**History anchors:** RCH §1.5 (both dates, figures documented).

### M15 — THE CRUELLEST YEAR
**Act III · 1913 · Smara–southern posts** · **RESISTANCE (Agdaf)** · Archetype: reprisal raids (tone-governed)
**Setup:** France has burned half-built Smara [RCH §1.6]. Agdaf answers. Three engagements are fixed: Liboirat (10 Jan, 60 French dead), Tagliat (10 Mar, 22 dead 37 wounded), Bou-Telis (10 Sep, 23 dead, 2 survivors) [RCH §1.6].
**Objectives:** Primary: strike all three posts. Secondary: take supplies, leave the wells standing (the war is for the land, not its death). **Tone rule: objectives are sabotage/capture — never kill-count; the mission names the historical dead in the outro, both sides'.** Hidden: spare the two Bou-Telis survivors (documented; they are the witnesses).
**Scripted events:** opening cinematic — Smara burning (still images + tbal drone); post-strike beats at the three documented dates; closing roll of names.
**Intro text:** *After the tbal prose:* "They burned the library that never finished being built. The drum answers." [RPY tbal, VERIFIED]
**Outro text:** *Narration.* "France called 1913 the year of the massacres. The desert called it the year the drum answered. Both sides buried their dead by name — so will this game."
**New mechanic:** reprisal tempo + tone system (no reward loop on deaths).
**History anchors:** RCH §1.6 (all three engagements, figures documented).

### M16 — THE LAST REZZOU
**Act III close · 1934 · the 6,000-kilometre arc** · **RESISTANCE (Reguibat)** · Archetype: grand raid / epilogue
**Setup:** The last great Reguibat rezzou: 6,000 km, 800 cattle, 270 camels taken, then intercepted [RCH §1.8]. Around you the noose closes: Tindouf occupied 31 May 1934, the emirate dead since '32, the méhariste deserters of '32 across the Spanish line.
**Objectives:** Primary: complete the raid arc — 6,000 km across the map's whole span, herds gathered. Secondary: disperse BEFORE interception (history: intercepted — choose the historical ending or the hidden one). Hidden: ride the arc home to the drums (the emir's tbal inherited — RPY).
**Scripted events:** odometer of the arc as HUD; French radio-net tightening (interception probability rises per week); final beat — the choice screen: historical interception (epilogue text below) or the unhistorical escape [FICTION alternative ending, clearly marked].
**Intro text:** *Raid count, narrated.* "Six thousand kilometres. Eight hundred cattle, two hundred and seventy camels. One last time, the Reguibat make the desert theirs from horizon to horizon."
**Outro text:** *Epilogue.* "On 31 May 1934 Tindouf fell and France declared the pacification complete — a sentence in a ledger, 1902–1934. But the drum was never silenced; it passed from hand to hand. The soil was never defeated." (tbal line after RPY; "undefeated on Mauritanian soil" after RCH §1.5.)
**New mechanic:** grand-raid arc + epilogue system (campaign closes).
**History anchors:** RCH §1.8 (raid figures, Tindouf date); RPY (tbal inheritance).
