### 1.3 The Char Bouba war — the era's defining war **[MAJOR HISTORIOGRAPHICAL CONFLICT]**
Two versions exist and they disagree on almost everything:

**Version A — the traditional "Mauritanian Thirty Years' War" (FR wiki, LOC country study, most French colonial-derived literature): 1644–1674.**
- 15th c.: Arab **Banu Maqfar/Banu Maqil** nomads push into the SW Sahara, extorting tribute from Zawaya (maraboutic Sanhaja) tribes; their southward expansion disrupts Adrar caravan routes
- 1644: war breaks out; Sanhadja/Zenaga confederation led by Imam **Nasr ad-Din** (born **Awbek b. Ashfaga**, a Lamtuna Zawaya scholar) resists the **Banu Hassan** (Yemeni-origin branch of Banu Maqil), commanded by **Sidi Ibrahim Al Aroussi** and **Sidi Tounsi Al Aroussi**
- Nasr ad-Din's commanders: **al-Amin, Uthman, Munir ad-Din** (FR article lists these as his war captains — EN treats them as his successors after his death; see conflict note)
- 1674: decisive defeat of the Sanhadja; **Treaty of Tin Yedfad** — the Sanhadja "permanently renounce arms"
- Result: Hassaniya arabization; the defeated split into learned tribes (zawaya/tolba) and tributaries (znaga)

**Version B — the revisionist reading (EN wiki Good Article, citing modern scholarship): a compact jihad **1673–1677/78**; the "1644–74 Thirty Years' War" framing is an error/poetic inflation by colonial historian **Paul Marty**, borrowed from hagiographies.**
- 1673: Nasr ad-Din enters **Futa Toro**, preaches the **toubenan** (repentance) movement; installs Islamic rulers: Jolof taken (Suranko enthroned at Ouarkhokh); in Cayor, **Lingeer Yacine Bubu** allies with qadi **Njaay Sall** to kill Damel Decce Maram; in Waalo, Brak **Fara Kumba Mbodj** killed, replaced by **Yerim Kode**
- 1674: Nasr imposes **zakat on warrior tribes**; the tributary **Bubba** calls in **Haddi b. Ahmad b. Daman of Trarza** — hence *Sharr Bubba*, "Bubba's war" (note: other readings derive the name from Haddi's own epithet)
- Battles: **Intuji** and **Jiwa** (Zawaya victories), then **Tirtilass** — a Zawaya tactical win but **Nasr ad-Din is killed** (by May 1674)
- Succession of doomed successors: **al-Faqih al-Amin** (deposed for compromising), **Qadi Uthman** (Nasr's vizier; killed near Guerouk in Biffeche ~May 1674 by Yerim Koode), **Nur al-Din** (killed at **Tin Yijmara**), **al-Mukhtar Agd 'Abdullah** (killed at **Amadir, 1677**)
- Wider war south of the river: French commander **De Muchins** raids Muslim villages upriver for slaves (May & July 1674); Jan 1676 **Ndogal** (Baol): Yerim Koode + Buurba Biram Penda defeat Suranko and Njaay Sall; Yacine Bubu beats Njaay Sall at **Xelere**; Wolof/Fula traditional aristocrats claw power back
- 1676: **devastating famine** — families sell themselves into slavery; slave trade booms
- Legacy: survivors' ideology seeds the Fula jihads (Bundu, Futa Jallon, Futa Toro) → Sokoto & the Toucouleur Empire (the 19th-century enemies of the emirates' descendants)

**Game call: use Version A's arc (1644→1674, "Thirty Years' War" — better campaign structure) but borrow Version B's dated battles and personalities for the final act (1673–77). Flag in the codex.**
- Settled consequences both versions agree on: Berbers "abandon the sword"; zawaya relinquish political/military power; **emirates of Trarza & Brakna founded (c. 1640s per Stewart / 1674 per EN Brakna)** with the winners on top; the social order of §2 hardens from **1674**

### 1.4 The emirates — foundings and rulers

**Brakna (1674–1903; capital Tiris per EN infobox). Founded by the Awlad Abdullah (al-Brakna) clan of Beni Hassan.**

- Awlad A'le line (1780–1903): Ahmadou I ibn Sidi B'kar (1780–1851), Sidi Amhamelsidi (1851–58), **Ahmadou II oualad A'le (1858–1903)** — signs French protectorate **Dec 1903** (per EN Brakna; the campaign report's "Brakna occupied 1904" = military phase)

Emir chosen by a **jema'a** of Hassane chieftaincies (Awlad Qaylan); the **Ahl Atman** lineage holds power from late 18th c. Seat: Atar.**

### 1.6 Waalo — the black-kingdom neighbor (diplo map)
1287 (trad.) foundation; Brak rulers; Jolof vassal till Danki 1549, token tribute till 1715; capitals Ndiourbel → Ndiangué → **Nder**. Women rulers matter: **Njembot Mbodj (1820–46)**, **Ndaté Yalla Mbodj (1846–55)**. Waalo taxed every boatload of gum/slaves for "protection"; 1762–65 trade war with Cayor; declined in favor of Trarza; French conquest 1855. Char Bouba's aftershock (Nasr ad-Din's death 1674) collapsed the marabout takeover here too.

## 2. SOCIETY — FACTIONS & UNITS

### 2.1 The class system (all "bidan"/white Moors except the bottom)
| Class | Role | Game mapping |
|---|---|---|
| **Hassane / Hassani** (from Banu Hassan) | Warrior aristocracy; political power; collect horma | Noble cavalry/camel lancer units; emirs |
| **Zawaya / Zwaya / tolba** (marabouts) | Religious + scholarly tribes; **paid no tribute**; religious authority, mediation, education; "abandoned the sword" after 1674 | Support cast: healers, imams, scholars; produce "baraka" resource; can't fight (mechanically: no weapons units) |
| **Znaga** | Tributaries; descendants of defeated Berber Sanhadja; pay **horma**; **forbidden arms** (Treaty of Tin Yedfad tradition) | Economy units: herders, gum harvesters |
| **Igaouen / ighyuwa** | Endogamous bard/griot caste (plus artisan castes — smiths claimed Semitic ancestry) | Morale/buff units; scouts |
| **Haratin** | Freed/servile black oasis cultivators | Oasis farming, date agriculture |
| **Abid** | Enslaved | Labor; can be raid objectives |

**Post-1674 legal details (from Char Bouba settlement):** Hassani won rights to **the milk of znaga herds, one third of well water, and three days' hospitality from each passing group** — extremely game-able tribute rules. Horma (EN wiki): personal tribute from each znaga member to a hassane overlord, "paid in cattle, goods or services," rates set by local power balance. LOC ch. 29: warrior–marabout **symbiosis** — warriors protect unarmed religious leaders, marabouts give political/spiritual legitimacy; alliance outlives French conquest. **Path to hassane status was merit-open:** the **Rguibat**, originally Zawaya Berbers, rose to hassane rank by taking up armed raiding (then Arabized) — great emergent-faction mechanic.

### 2.2 Warfare — the rezzou (razzia)
- From Arabic **ghazwa**: Bedouin camel-stealing incursion, "framed by rules and caused little bloodshed — enemy fighters were often left alive"; a resource-rebalancing institution, not slaughter **[FR Razzia]**
- **Rezzou** = the Moorish version (Moorish/Beidane/Zenaga/Tuareg practice on Saharan oases); French word "razzia" attested 1840–41, then pejoratively for their own massacres
- Enslaveable/raidable logic: polytheists = *kafir* = raidable; but by this era nearly everyone was Muslim — so raiding ran on tribal politics, horma contracts and tribute defaults, not religion
- **Seasonal rhythm:** gum harvest Dec–Jun ties raiding to the off-season; azalaï caravans run twice a year (Oct–Nov window for the Air route)
- **Scale template (from the 1857 Raid de Gandon, campaign report):** 400–500 men, ~50 horses, rest camels → horses are a small elite minority; the **camel lancer is the main battle unit**
- **Weapons:** lances & swords; no gunpowder tradition until the 19th c., when Saint-Louis merchants' **arms sales** create the 1870s–90s escalation (LOC ch. 10). Muskets/rifles = late-game tech unlock, literally imported from the enemy's merchants
- **Morale/siege quirks for missions:** the 3-month Idawish siege at Dhahr Tagant lifted for **40 horses**; ransoming European travelers was a revenue line 1870s–90s

## 3. ECONOMY — RESOURCES

### 3.1 Gum arabic (the headline trade)
- From **acacia senegal** (hard gum, *kitir* — the prized one; acacia seyal = friable *talha*); best gum lands = **Trarza & Brakna** (+ Ferlo); "better than Arabian gum" per early traders
- **Harvest (gommage):** tapping season **December–June**; ~250 g average yield/tree
- **Demand driver:** textile printing in Europe (and bazin finishing locally)
- **Mechanic — the coutume:** annual payment the Maures exact from any government/company wanting to trade; born of the Trarza amir's "substantial gifts" demand; Maures granted **simultaneous monopolies to France and the Dutch**; Faidherbe's 1860 treaties replaced it with a **3% rebate on gum delivered**; emirs reinstated it by force in the 1870s–90s
- Volume: Saint-Louis exports **doubled in the 1830s**
- Trading scene: "Maures traitant de la gomme à l'escale de Bakel" (1890 engraving) — the *escale* (river trading stop) is the natural market building

### 3.2 Salt
- **Idjil (Kédia d'Idjil, near modern Fderîck/Zouerate):** exploited since after mid-11th c.; **Ouadane** the staging post
- **Taoudeni → Timbuktu azalaï:** caravans of "a few hundred to several thousand camels", **twice a year**, ~900–1,000 km, **~3 weeks one way**; salt slabs **~25 kg each**, **2–4 slabs per camel**; return loads historically included slaves; organized/dominated by the **Kunta confederation** (with Tuareg muscle; miners at Taoudeni in servile status)
- Salt = the classic gold-for-salt trans-Saharan commodity; still worked at Kediet Ijill in the 1980s (LOC)

### 3.3 The rest
- **Dates:** Tagant/Adrar oases (Atar, Ouadane, Tidjikja — Tidjikja still runs a dates festival); oasis agriculture by haratin labor; date-palm groves = siege objectives (see Dhahr Tagant)
- **Camels & cattle:** the nomad wealth base; camels = currency of rezzou; the Chemama river pasture zone = 19th-c. Trarza war objective
- **Slaves:** raiding south (Waalo, Futa), the 1676 famine self-sales, azalaï return loads, 15th-c. Arguin exports (1,000/yr); LOC/Precolonial note the Atlantic trade corrupted the Wolof states and helped trigger Char Bouba
- **Gold:** gone as the Mauritanian-route staple — post-1591 Songhai collapse + Portuguese/Moroccan diversions pushed trade to the coasts (LOC ch. 8: routes shifted east, Mauritanian towns declined); Ouadane's Portuguese entrepôt (1487) was the last gasp
- **Weights trivia:** 25-kg salt slab; 2–4 slabs/camel; 40 horses as a siege's price; "3 days' hospitality" and "⅓ of well water" as legal tribute units

## 6. TRIVIA & TUTORIAL-MISSION CANDIDATES (best dramatic material)
1. **1480 — Mossi sack of Oualata.** Besieged a month; the town falls, but inhabitants regroup, pursue the raiders and **rescue the captives** — a ready-made "defense → counter-raid → rescue" tutorial.
2. **1591 — Tondibi.** 4-month desert march scenario (water/attrition mechanic), then the battle where the **cattle stampede backfires** onto its owners. Perfect prologue: teaches that gunpowder changes everything.
3. **1644/1673–77 — Char Bouba finale.** Nasr ad-Din's jihad arc: win at Intuji and Jiwa, win-but-die at **Tirtilass**, then play his four successors to their deaths (Tin Yijmara, Amadir 1677). Sub-mechanic: zakat imposed on warrior tribes.
4. **1676 — the famine.** Survival scenario: families selling themselves into slavery (dark; tone carefully).
5. **1678 — Moulay Ismail's tour of the south.** Play a Banu Maqil tribal delegation: submit and pay gharama, or resist and lose the forts game.
6. **1719 — Brakna invades Trarza.** Exile-to-Morocco mission; return with Makhzen auxiliaries; dynastic revenge.
7. **3-month siege of Dhahr Tagant (mid-18th c.)** — lifted for **40 horses**: teaches "sieges end in negotiation" and the horse-wealth economy.
8. **The azalaï escort.** Timbuktu↔Taoudeni, ~25-kg slabs, 2–4 per camel, 3 weeks, twice a year, Kunta logistics vs rezzou ambush.
9. **Gum-market tutorial (any 18th-c. year).** Play the Trarza amir auctioning simultaneous monopolies to France and the Dutch; collect the coutume; cheat both.
10. **1825/1833 — the Waalo marriage gambit.** Njembot Mbodj's diplomacy vs the emir vs Saint-Louis: three-sided negotiation scenario ending in the Dagana wedding (18 Jun 1833) and the Pujol treaty (30 Aug 1835).
11. **1660 — founding of Tidjikja.** Post-war settlement builder: plant the palm groves, dig the wells, start the library.
12. **Cross-ref from campaign report:** Pont de Leybar 1855, Raid de Gandon 15–16 May 1857 (the canonical rezzou template).
