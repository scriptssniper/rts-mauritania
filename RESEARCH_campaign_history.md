## 2. FACTIONS & LEADERS

### 2.1 French side
**Political/conquest:**
- **Xavier Coppolani** (1866–1905), Corsican Arabist, "the Pacific Conqueror" / *le charmeur* — conquer by sermon, treaty and subsidy; doctrine: "In Muslim lands, the word religion replaces the word patrie." Assassinated 12 May 1905 (see 1.2). His tomb at Tidjikja: "the friend of the Muslims."
- **Henri Gouraud** (1867–1946), colonel, commissioner from 1907 — military method, camel columns, counter-rezzou; future Gallipoli/Levant commander and military governor of Paris. Wrote the primary source memoir (*La Pacification de la Mauritanie*, 1910).
- Supporting officers: **Capt. Frèrejean** (post-builder incl. Nouakchott; succeeded Coppolani; led 1909 reinforcements), **Lt. Etiévent** (killer of Coppolani's assassin), **Capt. Repona** (killed 16 Mar 1908), **Bablon** (killed 1909), **Capt. Gaston Fourn** (Senegal spahis squadron, 1908 campaign), **Col. Trinquet** (Tindouf 1934), Gens. Moinier/Mangin/Giraud (Morocco/late period).

**Units:**
- **Tirailleurs sénégalais** — the backbone; 1er RTS kept 2 battalions in Mauritania (Port-Étienne & Kaédi) by 1911; drapeau battle honors "Mauritanie 1902" then **"Mauritanie 1904–1913"**. Mounted on camels for desert columns.
- **Spahis sénégalais** — Saint-Louis-based cavalry squadron (Fourn).
- **Méharistes / groupes nomades** — camel corps; the Compagnies Méharistes Sahariennes model dates to 1902 (Algerian Sahara, Chaamba recruits), transposed into Mauritania as mobile "police of the desert." White/blue gandouras, light-blue képis for French, barefoot riding.
- **Foreign Legion — essentially absent from Mauritania** (sources never place Legion units there; Legion enters Saharan companies only from 1940). **[UNCERTAIN — don't build a Legion faction]**
- Artillery: negligible — a couple of light mountain pieces per column at most **[UNCERTAIN — exact numbers not in retrieved sources]**.

### 2.2 Resistance side
**The Fadiliyya house (the central dynasty of the war):**
- **Cheikh Ma El Aïnin** (b. 10 Feb 1831 near Oualata **[variants: 1830/1838]**; d. 23 Oct 1910 Tiznit). 12th of 48 sons of Muhammad Fâdil ben Mâmîn, founder of the Fadiliyya (Qadiri branch). Hajj 1858; sultan's delegate over Souss→Saguia el Hamra from 1879; built **Smara** (~1898–1902, library, arms store); proclaimed jihad 1904; armed by sultans Abdelaziz then Abdelhafid (the sultan's steamship *Turki*, commanded by a German officer, **Leonhard Karow**, ran grain and guns to Tarfaya); fled Smara 1908–09 → Tiznit, proclaimed himself **Mahdi**; broken at Tadla 23 Jun 1910. ~140 books attributed; later canonized in Le Clézio's novel *Désert*.
- **Ahmed al-Hiba ("the Blue Sultan")** (c.1876–1919) — son; Sultan at Tiznit 1912, took Marrakech 14 Aug 1912, crushed at Sidi Bou Othmane 6 Sep 1912; died Kerdous 23 Jun 1919.
- **Merebbi Rebbu (Murabbih Rabbuh)** — son; successor as resistance chief 1919, in the Saguia el-Hamra.
- **Agdaf** — son; led the 1913 reprisal that killed ~40 French.
- (Other sons: Muhammad al-Aghzaf, al-Wali, Muhammad Taqi Allah…)

**Emirates (hassane warrior states):**
- **Adrar** — the fortress emirate. Emirs: Shaykh Al-Hasana (1899–1909); **Sidi Ahmed Ould Ahmed Ayda** (1909–13; captured, restored 1913 as puppet, dissident 1931, d. 1932 — the tragedy arc). Capital Atar; cities Chinguetti, Ouadane.
- **Trarza** (ends 1902/1909) and **Brakna** (ends 1903/1910) — broken early by Coppolani's political method, emirs neutralized without major battles.
- **Tagant** — emirate capital **Tidjikja** (founded ~1660 by the Idaw Ali); the pivot region 1905–1907.
- **Hodh** — eastern emirate, Kounta networks (Kounta chief Mohamed El Mokhtar Ould Hamed **of Rachid** — the town the French razed); administered from Bamako until 1939.

**Tribes:** warrior/haughty: **Rguibat (Reguibat)** — the irreconcilables (sacked Tindouf 1896 with 1,200 men after a 7-day battle; raided Port-Étienne 1924/1927; deserted en masse 1932; the final 1934 rezzou), **Ouled Delim**, Tekna, **Tenaki** (Coppolani's assassins), Ouled Ghar. Tributary (**znaga**) tribes paid the hassane in military service and goods — the *coutume* the French exploited then "banned."

**Maraboutic alignments — CRITICAL NUANCE for faction design:**
- The war was **NOT Tijaniyya vs Qadiriyya**. Both main maraboutic networks were **Qadiriyya**:
  - **Qadiriyya-Sidiyya (Cheikh Sidiya Baba**, Boutilimit) — pro-French, allied to Coppolani; influence Trarza/Brakna/Tagant/Adrar.
  - **Qadiriyya-Fadiliyya** — split down the family: **Cheikh Saad Bouh** (1848–1917, Nimjat/Touizikt) = pro-French pacifist, mediator (saved explorer Soleillet 1880; wrote the anti-jihad *Nasîha* 1906; 664 sheikhs consecrated; vast Senegal network) vs his elder brother **Ma El Aïnin** = jihadist. The Goudfiya (Ma El Aïnin's own network, strong in Tagant/Adrar/Hodh) is the resistance's religious arm.
  - **Tijaniyya** in Mauritania = Omariya/Hamallya branches, recruited among **black Africans** (Toucouleur, Soninké) of the river — mostly outside this campaign's Moorish war, though relevant to the southern flank. (LOC, ch. 39)
- In game terms: the true religious civil war is **within one family and one brotherhood** — Saad Bouh vs Ma El Aïnin, uncle vs uncle by proxy.

## 3. MILITARY ASYMMETRY — MECHANIC-READY DETAIL

### French strengths / methods
- **The borj (fort-post) lattice:** Nouakchott (1904), Tidjikja/Fort Coppolani, Moudjeria, Inchiri (1908), Atar, Chami, Bou-Telis, Port-Étienne (named 1907), Kaédi, Bir Moghrein (**Fort-Trinquet**, facing Spanish Guelta Zemmour). Sieges of these posts = natural defense missions (Fort Coppolani held ~25 days in Nov 1906; Tidjikja defended again Nov 1907).
- **Camel columns (colonnes)**: 700+ camels for the Adrar column; tirailleurs mounted à dos de chameau; the column is a moving fortress with its own water discipline.
- **Counter-rezzou** raids mirroring the enemy's own tactic (Gouraud's explicit method).
- **Divide-and-rule diplomacy:** subsidies, the *coutume*→rebate switch, restoration of puppet emirs (1913), 800+ recognized chiefs and sub-chiefs (LOC) — the "peaceful penetration" tech tree.
- **Information warfare:** the Tangier arabophone paper **Es-Saada** ran a character-assassination campaign painting Ma El Aïnin as a mendicant and smuggler (en wiki) — propaganda mechanic, historically real.
- Weaknesses: water (11 tirailleurs died of thirst after Nouarmach, Jul 1908), heat, distance (Moudjeria→Adrar vacuum), tiny European officer cadre targeted by snipers/ambushes (Oglat-Saiet 1908, Niémilane 1907…), Saint-Louis merchants selling arms to the enemy (an internal scandal mechanic).

### Mauritanian strengths / methods
- **Rezzou (razzia) doctrine:** fast camel converging raids for livestock, slaves and prestige; template = Gandon 1857 (400–500 riders, 50 horses, 80 captives; escape before spahi pursuit). The 1934 Reguibat "grand rezzou": **6,000 km**, 800 cattle, 270 camels, 10 slaves.
- **Well control:** the desert fights over water, not terrain; deny wells to columns = kill them slowly.
- **Natural fortresses:** Atar and the Adrar ksour; the Hamdoun pass as the single gate (forced 8 Jan 1909).
- **Moroccan logistics:** sultan's arsenals, the *Turki* steamship line to Tarfaya, later German/Turkish WWI supply (1916 submarine, Proebster mission); rifle cartridges (Mauser/Remington types) flowing south **[specific rifle models beyond cartridges are [UNCERTAIN] in retrieved sources — Remington Rolling Blocks are the historiographical standard for Saharan warriors]**.
- Weaknesses: no artillery, no siege train (they besiege but rarely take posts), tribal fragmentation (Coppolani's opening), dependence on one charismatic lineage, drought/herd depletion (1912–34 raiding was largely herd-replenishment economics — LOC).

### Economic layer
- Gum arabic (Trarza), salt caravans (azalaï), slaves, cattle, camels; tribute (*coutume*) as the emirates' tax base; the 3% rebate treaty; arms traffic as the merchants' profit line. Herd destruction 1905–12 → the 1912–34 raid economy.

## 4. GEOGRAPHY (map skeleton)

**Regions (W→E, S→N):**
- **Senegal valley (Chemama):** Saint-Louis (colonial capital 1903–57), Podor, Rosso, Bogué, Kaédi — the French base-line.
- **Trarza:** coast + gum belt; Nouakchott (post, 1904), Boutilimit (Sidiya Baba), Nimjat (Saad Bouh); the old Walo (Waalo) bridgehead.
- **Brakna:** river steppe; Aleg.
- **Tagant:** plateau; **Tidjikja** (Fort Coppolani; capital of the Tagant emirate, founded ~1660 by Idaw Ali; 5,000+ ancient manuscripts), Moudjeria (column base), **Oujeft/Aoujeft** (oasis on the Adrar road), Boumdeït (Chadeliya center).
- **Adrar:** the fortress plateau; **Atar** (garrison town, taken 9 Jan 1909), **Chinguetti** (7th holy city, library city, Ma El Aïnin's 1910 departure point), **Ouadane**, Kédia d'Idjil (iron mountain; later Fort-Gouraud/Zouerate), Hamdoun pass (the gate), Bou-Telis.
- **Hodh (Chargui/Gharbi):** Oualata (Ma El Aïnin's birth region; manuscript city), Néma, Tichfit, Rachid (razed), Tichit — long ruled from Bamako.
- **Inchiri:** Akjoujt corridor; the 1908 forward post.
- **Northern borderland / Spanish Sahara:** **Cap Juby** (MacKenzie's post, seized ~1898; Villa Bens 1916), **Smara** (Ma El Aïnin's unfinished capital, partly destroyed 1913), Guelta Zemmour (Spanish), **Bir Moghrein** (Fort-Trinquet), **Tindouf** (taken 31 May 1934), **Port-Étienne/Nouadhibou** (Atlantic; attacked 1924 & 1927).
- **Morocco (endgame maps):** Tiznit, Tadla/Kasba Zidania (1910), Marrakech & Sidi Bou Othmane (1912), Kerdous.

**Water/terrain notes for map design:** wells as capture points; palmery belts (Tidjikja assassination site); passes as chokes (Hamdoun); the Senegal ferry crossings (Rosso, Mékinah); imensity gaps (Moudjeria→Adrar) where nothing lives but rezzou paths.

## 5. GAME-RELEVANT TRIVIA & DRAMATIC BEATS

1. **The assassination minute-by-minute (12 May 1905):** a dream-prophecy sharif, 27 Tenaki warriors, a five-minute firefight (21h25–21h30) at the milk delivery after night prayer; Coppolani's last words: "These wretches have killed me — they do not deserve me"; Lt. Etiévent pinning the assassin's corpse to the ground with its own saber; the lone survivor hanged. A mission designed around a 5-minute window.
2. **The two-brother duel:** Saad Bouh's *Nasîha* (1906) — a theological anti-jihad letter to the very man waging it; both are sons of the same saint. Diplomacy mechanic: one family, two win conditions.
3. **Thirst as a unit stat:** Nouarmach (24 Jul 1908) — 13 killed in the fight, 11 more died of thirst afterward.
4. **The Fort Coppolani month-long siege (Nov 1906, [CONFLICT with Nov 1907])** — a small mixed garrison vs the dissident Tagant.
5. **1913, the year of three massacres:** Liboirat (60 French dead), Tagliat (22+37), Bou-Telis (23 dead, 2 survivors) — triggered by the burning of Smara and Agdaf's vengeance. Perfect "dark middle" of act three.
6. **The puppet-emir arc:** Sidi Ahmed Ould Ahmed Ayda — captured (1909/10), restored (1913), dissident again (1931), dead in the desert (1932). Tragedy written in one life.
7. **Foreign interference as resource lanes:** sultan's *Turki* steamship (with German captain Karow) → guns; 1916 German submarine at the Oued Draa mouth (captured by Spain); Es-Saada press war from Tangier.
8. **The unfinished city:** Smara — a holy capital never completed, its library and walls half-built, burned by the French in 1913, still a ruin-today. Iconic RTS "wonder" objective.
9. **Rezzou economics:** the 1934 Reguibat 6,000-km raid (800 cattle, 270 camels, 10 slaves) as the final "great hunt" scenario; the 1857 Gandon raid (80 captives, 8-man blockhaus stand at Nder) as the mechanics template.
10. **Tech gap, honestly:** no Mauritanian artillery vs French mountain guns (rare), Remington/Gras-era rifles on both sides, camels vs rail/river steamers on the Senegal — the gap is **logistics and forts**, not weapons quality; that's the game's true asymmetry.
11. **Coppolani's epitaph**, in Arabic, on his tomb at Tidjikja: *"the friend of the Muslims."* Gouraud's doctrine quote for the opposite doctrine: "It is not a matter of conquering the Adrar for permanent occupation… but of organizing a police column to purge the country."
12. **Cultural touchstone:** Le Clézio's Prix Renaudot novel *Désert* (1980) follows Ma El Aïnin's people — the campaign has a literary canon.
13. **Ouineik: NOT FOUND.** No French/English Wikipedia article, Britannica entry, LOC chapter, or web index contains a "Battle of Ouineik." The engagement most likely intended is **Oujeft/Aoujeft** (Tagant-Adrar road oasis) or a garbled transliteration of another Tagant combat (candidates: Oglat-Saiet 1908, Amatil Dec 1908, or the forcing of Hamdoun Jan 1909). **Treat any Ouineik scenario as fiction unless a primary source (Gouraud's 1910 journal, on Gallica) names it.**
14. **Last shot of the era:** the final combat against the Rguibat came only in 1958 (Opération Écouvillon) per Col. Ould Beibecar — the "pacification" of 1934 was administrative, not emotional.
